## 2. The queue's semantics, as invariants

- **`CRG-1` — IT HOLDS THE ADDRESS AND NEVER A READING.** A queue of values would be a SECOND AUTHORITY beside the record; what is held is the delivery's own argument. **The flush therefore translates nothing**: it re-emits the crossing's one signal and the renderer re-reads.
- **`CRG-2` — THE FLUSH HAPPENS ONCE, UNCONDITIONALLY.** `arm()` pushes whether or not anything was held, because a queue that flushed only with something held would leave a RELOADED realm unpainted until the next record change — the exact silence this crossing exists to remove (`RR-B2-3`).
- **`CRG-3` — THE CLEAR IS UNCONDITIONAL TOO**, and it runs after the push; the `armed` flag is set FIRST so nothing delivered DURING the push is held behind it.
- **`CRG-4` — `held` IS A COPY**: an insertion-ordered reading for a battery, never a destination, and nothing under `src/` reads it.
- **`CRG-5` — `deliver` IS TOTAL AND THROWS NOTHING**, and it runs no store verb: an uncontained throw at a registration instant refuses the construction at the store's rank 21, and a listener body's write would open a commit inside the one that fired it.
- **`CRG-6` — NO TIMER, NO POLL, NO RETRY.** `D-82` clause 4's discipline, one surface over: the flush rides the realm's own arrival, one arm per arrival; a hold that also polled would be a second arrival route for one event.
- **`CRG-7` — THE NAMES ARE PINNED, NOT DISCOVERED**: `DeliveryQueue`, `createDeliveryQueue`, `deliver`, `arm`, `held`, and the instances `pushDisplayChanged`/`displayQueue`/`displayDelivery`/`displayStoreHolder`/`displayStore`. Arm `(c)` resolves the first five out of the extracted bytes and the lockstep rows assert the last five by name.
- **`CRG-8` — THE PUSH IS BEST-EFFORT AND THE CLEAR IS NOT** (`B2-ADV-03`, landed in this pass). `CRG-2`/`CRG-3` are only invariants if the flush cannot be aborted from inside the push: `arm()` sets the flag, pushes, and clears — so the push's own body must not be able to throw out of it, or the clear becomes SKIPPABLE BY A SINK. Hence `pushDisplayChanged` fans out with the destroyed guard **and** contains each `webContents.send` in its own `try`/`catch`: a destroyed or racing `webContents` may refuse the signal (that is the sink's business, answered by the next arrival's re-read), one window's refusal must not cost the other windows their signal, and **the arity is unchanged** — `send(DISPLAY_CHANGED)`, no second argument, because the containment changes the failure mode and not the payload.

## 3. Why the arm sits inside the existing `IPC_READY` body, after `markReady()`

Two orderings, each load-bearing:

1. **AFTER `backend.markReady()`** — the push's consumer answers with a `display.read()` invoke, and the backend dispatches an invoke only once it is ready: a flush issued before that gate would hand the realm a signal whose read has not been armed.
2. **INSIDE the existing body, as its second statement** — a second `ipcMain.on(IPC_READY, …)` registration would be a second arrival route for one event, and the renderer subscribes **above** `bridge.ready()` (§6) precisely so this flush finds its listener standing. `IPC_READY` is the app's own arrival signal; `attachWindow`, one line after `new BrowserWindow`, is the site that does NOT work — the renderer process does not exist yet and `webContents.send` is discarded.

## 4. The degradation arm — what a refused construction does, and what it may never do

The seam constructs the store (`src/main/storeroom.ts`, `createProfileStore`) over a `filePath` from `app.getPath('userData')`; a `filePath` that can never be occupied is refused BY THE PACKAGE, and the foundation funnels that into a FATAL boot and left the degrade-or-die question OPEN (contract §2 quotes it). **Here it degrades**, and the reason is D-23's payload: display state is a caret and a scroll, and a lost caret must not kill an app whose document content lives elsewhere.

- **What it DOES**: one `console.error` carrying the store's own `status` and the path; `displayStore` stays `null`; `ipcMain.handle(DISPLAY_READ, …)` answers `null`.
- **WHAT IT DOES NOT DO, corrected by measurement (`B2-ADV-01`)**: it does **not** make the renderer report `no-port`. `no-port` is the ABSENT-`display`-MEMBER arm (`src/renderer/display-client.ts`'s `member === null` test) and a preload that always exposes the member can never produce it; the degraded boot answers **`subscribed: true` with a `null` reading**. **A consumer keys on the READING, never on a `reason` the port cannot spell.**
- **AND THE CODE'S OWN PROSE STILL SAYS IT, OWED, NOT FIXED HERE**: `main.ts`'s degrade-arm comment **and** the `console.error` template it emits both claim the renderer "reports `no-port`". The behaviour is right and the words are wrong; this pass's two-fix scope (the push's containment, the renderer's docblock) did not reach them, and they are named here so the contradiction is not discovered by a later grep.
- **What it FORBIDS, and the census `(f)` is what measures it**: a second construction, a defaulted `filePath`, a retry, a fallback store. ## 5. The push's arity, and the preload's structural twin

- **THE PUSH CARRIES NOTHING**: `target.webContents.send(DISPLAY_CHANGED)` — ONE argument, no address, no value, and `CRG-8`'s `try`/`catch` wraps it without adding one. A second argument would be a payload the renderer would have to ignore, and the reading is an ARITY so a comment cannot satisfy it.
- **THE PRELOAD'S `display` MEMBER IS ONE TOP-LEVEL KEY WITH EXACTLY TWO MEMBERS, NEITHER A SENDER** (`src/main/preload.ts`, `ProvidentBridge` and the exposed literal): `read()` invokes `DISPLAY_READ` and answers the record's reading; `onChanged(handler)` registers ONE `ipcRenderer.on(DISPLAY_CHANGED, listener)` whose body calls `handler()` at **arity zero with nothing**, and returns `() => ipcRenderer.removeListener(<same channel>, <same listener>)`.
- **THE TWIN IS THE PRELOAD'S OWN**: `read(): Promise<Record<string, unknown> | null>`, declared on ONE line. It is NOT the renderer's `DisplayReading` and NOT a type from `src/main/record-engine/` — either import would invert the realm boundary and admit a second authority on the entry shape (`ASM-B2-1`; the `SecurityWriteReceipt` precedent). Both channel names come from `src/main/store-channels.ts` (F-8); the member spells neither literal.

## 6. The renderer site: subscribe, THEN one read, ABOVE `bridge.ready()`

`src/renderer/renderer.ts` — `DisplayBridge`/`DisplayCrossing`/`installDisplayCrossing`, and the ONE call site above `bridge.ready()`.

- **SUBSCRIBE FIRST, THEN `fill()` ONCE**: a read taken before the subscription loses every change that lands between the two, and the listener re-reads on every arrival anyway. `fill()` is called **only where the subscription succeeded** — a fill over a port with no read route would be a read the boot made against a route that is not there.
- **THE SITE MUST PRECEDE `bridge.ready()`** because that signal is what arms main's queue (§3): a subscription registered after it would miss the flush it is the destination of.
- **TOTALITY IS THE CLIENT'S** and the install adds nothing to it: no bridge, no `display` member, a member that is not an object, a `read` that is not a function, an `onChanged` that throws on registration — each is a DECLARED `subscribed: false` with its OWN word and a total `release`.
- **THE BINDING IS HELD, AND THERE IS EXACTLY ONE SUBSCRIPTION PER REALM** (`B2-ADV-04`). The site's `const displayCrossing = installDisplayCrossing(…)` is this realm's whole share of the crossing for the realm's life: the `client` and the ONE subscription (`ipcRenderer.on` registration) inside it. **`B-3`/`B-10` LIFT that binding — or take the crossing from the `client` they already hold — and MUST NEVER call `installDisplayCrossing` a second time**, because a second call is a second `ipcRenderer.on` registration for ONE channel: a double subscription, a double re-read per push, and a listener nothing withdraws. It stays a LOCAL `const`, not a module-scope export, because nothing at this site renders.
- **IT PAINTS NOTHING** (`D-8`, `RR-F1`): the handler is the client's own re-read; the crossing is HELD for `B-3`, which owns the channel that renders the reading. No `state-slice`, no `mount`, no DOM write, no graph install.
